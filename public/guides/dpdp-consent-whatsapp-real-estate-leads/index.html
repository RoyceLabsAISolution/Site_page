DPDP Act and WhatsApp real-estate leads: a consent checklist

By Royce Labs · Last updated 14 November 2026

Short answer: Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, consent for personal data must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action, and the person must be able to withdraw it as easily as they gave it. For WhatsApp property leads, that means saying who you are, why you want the buyer's details, asking a clear yes or no before saving anything, and honouring STOP.

Not legal advice. This checklist is general information written by a software company, and it has not been reviewed by a lawyer. The Rules under the Act are being phased in, and details can change. Confirm anything you rely on with qualified counsel or the official text.

Where the law stands

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India's data protection law. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified in November 2025. Published summaries describe a phased rollout over about 18 months from notification, with the main obligations on businesses expected to apply around May 2027. Check the exact dates in the official text; do not rely on this page for them.

What "consent" must look like

  • Free and unconditional: do not make an unrelated service depend on agreeing to extra data use.
  • Specific and informed: tell the buyer what you will save and why (for example, "your name, number, budget and area, so our team can follow up about plots"), before you ask.
  • Unambiguous, with a clear affirmative action: a clear "YES" in the chat. Silence, "maybe" or a pre-ticked box is not consent.
  • Withdrawable: stopping should be as easy as agreeing. A simple "STOP" should end messaging and trigger deletion or restriction as your policy says.
  • Notice: the Act and Rules expect a clear notice in plain language, and in some cases in the languages in the Constitution's Eighth Schedule, which includes Tamil. Ask counsel what applies to you.

A practical checklist for WhatsApp property leads

  1. Say in the first reply that the buyer is talking to an automated assistant.
  2. Ask consent before saving a name, number or requirement, and say what for.
  3. Treat anything other than a clear yes as no.
  4. Stop messaging on STOP or "no", and record that consent was withdrawn.
  5. Store the consent status and time with each lead.
  6. Collect only what you need. Do not collect ID documents or money in the chat.
  7. Do not message people who never contacted you without a lawful basis your lawyer has checked.
  8. Decide who is responsible for the data (you as the developer or agency, and any vendor acting for you) and put it in writing.
  9. Have a way for buyers to ask what you hold and to ask you to delete it.
  10. Keep a plain-language privacy notice and link to it.

FAQ

Do I need consent to reply to a buyer who messaged me first?
Replying to someone who contacted you is ordinary. Saving and using their personal data is where consent or another lawful basis comes in. Ask your lawyer which basis applies to your case; our assistant asks for consent before saving anything.
Does consent have to be in Tamil?
The Rules expect notices to be clear and plain, and in some cases to be available in languages in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, which includes Tamil. Our assistant replies in English or Tamil to match the buyer. Confirm what applies to you with counsel.
