Tamil Nadu property terms explained: patta, chitta, DTCP, CMDA, RERA, cent and ground

By Royce Labs · Last updated 13 November 2026

Short answer: Tamil Nadu property chats mix English and Tamil and use local terms: patta and chitta (land records), EC (encumbrance certificate), DTCP and CMDA (planning authorities), TNRERA (the real-estate regulator), cent and ground (units of land), and lakh and crore (amounts). This glossary explains each one in plain language.

Plain-language explanations for buyers and for people building tools for them. They are not legal advice. Check official sources for any property you are buying.

Land records and documents

Patta
A revenue record from the Tamil Nadu revenue department that shows who is recorded as holding a piece of land, used for land tax. A clear patta in the seller's name is one of the first things buyers ask about.
Chitta
An extract from village land records that shows details such as the survey number, extent, land type and the recorded holder. Buyers often ask to see the patta and chitta together.
EC (Encumbrance Certificate)
A certificate from the Registration department listing registered transactions and charges on a property over a period. Buyers use it to check for registered mortgages or earlier sales.
FMB (Field Measurement Book) sketch
A survey sketch of a land parcel showing its boundaries and measurements.
Guideline value
The government's minimum value used to calculate stamp duty and registration charges. It can differ from the market price.

Approvals and regulators

DTCP
The Directorate of Town and Country Planning, Tamil Nadu. In practice, buyers ask whether a layout is "DTCP approved" outside the Chennai Metropolitan Area.
CMDA
The Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority, which handles planning permission within the Chennai Metropolitan Area. Chennai buyers ask whether a project is "CMDA approved".
TNRERA
The Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory Authority. Under the RERA rules, projects with more than 500 square metres of land or more than eight apartments, counting all phases, must be registered before sale, and registered projects have a RERA registration number. You can check registered projects on rera.tn.gov.in.

Units and amounts

Cent
A unit of land area. 1 cent is about 435.6 square feet. 100 cents make 1 acre.
Ground
A unit of land area used especially in Chennai. 1 ground is 2,400 square feet.
Square foot (sq ft)
The usual unit for built-up area and, increasingly, for plots.
Lakh
100,000. A budget of "40 lakh" is ₹40,00,000.
Crore
10,000,000 (100 lakh). A budget of "1.2 crore" is ₹1,20,00,000.

Language

Tanglish
Tamil typed in English letters, often mixed with English words, for example "3 cent plot Coimbatore la irukka?". Many property enquiries on WhatsApp arrive this way.

FAQ

What is the difference between patta and chitta?
Patta is the revenue record of who holds the land, used for land tax. Chitta is an extract from village records with details such as survey number, extent and land type. Buyers often ask for both.
How big is a cent and a ground?
1 cent is about 435.6 square feet, and 100 cents make an acre. 1 ground is 2,400 square feet and is used especially in Chennai.
Which projects need TNRERA registration?
Projects with more than 500 square metres of land or more than eight apartments, counting all phases, must be registered before sale. Check registered projects on rera.tn.gov.in.
