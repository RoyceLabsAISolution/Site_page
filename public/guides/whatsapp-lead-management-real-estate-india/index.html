WhatsApp lead management for real estate in India: a practical guide

By Royce Labs · Last updated 14 November 2026

Short answer: WhatsApp lead management means replying to property enquiries on WhatsApp quickly, capturing what the buyer wants, and handing a ready lead to a person. For Indian developers and agencies, the practical rules are: get the buyer's opt-in, reply in the buyer's language, never let automation quote prices or take money, and keep a person in charge of site visits.

Why WhatsApp for property enquiries in India

Most property enquiries in India arrive as a message: from an ad, a site board, a portal listing or a referral. Buyers tend to message several developers and stay with the one that answers first and clearly. A team on site visits cannot reply to every message within minutes, so enquiries wait until the next day.

What to automate, and what to keep human

AutomateKeep with a person
First reply, in the buyer's languagePrice quotes and negotiation

Capturing budget, location, size, timelineConfirming a site visit

Opt-in, the 24-hour window and templates

On the WhatsApp Business Platform, a business can reply freely while the buyer's conversation is active, which Meta calls the customer service window and sets at 24 hours from the buyer's last message. After that window, a business generally needs an approved message template, and WhatsApp's policies expect the person to have opted in to hear from you. This is why a first-reply system for buyers who message you first is simpler and safer than outbound campaigns. Check Meta's current policy and pricing, as both change.

Language matters more than people expect

Buyers in Tamil Nadu often write Tamil in English letters (Tanglish), mix in English, and use units like cents and grounds and amounts like lakh and crore. A system that only understands formal English will mishandle many ordinary enquiries. Ask any vendor to show you a real Tanglish conversation, not a slide.

Ten questions to ask a WhatsApp lead-management vendor

  1. Does it tell the buyer it is automated?
  2. Does it ask consent before saving a name, number or requirement?
  3. What happens when the buyer says STOP or "no"?
  4. Can it quote a price or invent a listing? (It should not.)
  5. Which languages does it actually handle, and can you see them live?
  6. Who owns the lead data, and can you export it?
  7. Does a person confirm every site visit?
  8. What has been proven with real buyers, and what has only been tested offline?
  9. Is there a lock-in, and what does it cost after any pilot?
  10. Who fixes it when something breaks?

Our own answers: the assistant says it is automated, asks consent first, stops on STOP or "no", never quotes prices or invents listings, handles English and Tamil, and a person confirms every visit. It is built and tested offline; live WhatsApp with real buyers is not yet proven, and the Coimbatore pilot is its first live run.

FAQ

Is WhatsApp lead management legal in India?
Using WhatsApp to reply to people who message you is ordinary business practice. What matters is how you handle their personal data, which falls under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and WhatsApp's own business policies. This is general information, not legal advice; ask a lawyer about your own setup.
Can a bot quote a price or take a booking amount?
It should not. Prices, negotiation, booking amounts and ID documents should stay with a person on your team.
Do I need the WhatsApp Business Platform, or is the Business app enough?
The Business app suits one person replying manually. Automation, multiple team members and a lead console generally need the WhatsApp Business Platform. Check Meta's current rules and pricing.
